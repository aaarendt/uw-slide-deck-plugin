SENSITIVE AND REGULATED DATA. The question we get at every talk: can I put my data in a CloudBank account? Yes, sensitive data can go in the cloud, but a standard account is not set up for it. Whether you can, and how, depends on the rules that come with your data, not on the cloud. Know your data's rules. Health records covered by HIPAA (protected health information) need a business associate agreement, a signed contract in which the cloud provider agrees to protect the data, and it has to cover the specific account you use. Your university may have one with AWS or Microsoft for accounts it owns; do not assume it covers a CloudBank account. Health data can then only go into the provider's HIPAA-eligible services. NIH controlled-access human genomic data (for example from dbGaP): since January 25, 2025, NIH requires that it be kept on systems, including cloud systems, that meet the federal security standard NIST SP 800-171, and you attest to this when you sign or renew the data use certification. Many genomics researchers don't realise this applies to them. Federal controlled unclassified information follows the same standard; export-controlled data (ITAR, EAR) can need a government cloud and access only by U.S. persons. Student records (FERPA), licensed or proprietary data, and anything covered by an IRB protocol or data use agreement carry whatever conditions that agreement sets. Before you upload. Read the rules first. Then ask your institution: the privacy office, the IRB or research security; many universities already have approved, compliant cloud environments. Then talk to CloudBank as early as you can. CloudBank supports regulated data through Sherlock at the San Diego Supercomputer Center, which runs managed environments on AWS, Azure and Google Cloud built for HIPAA, federal (FISMA, controlled unclassified) and FERPA requirements, with access control, encryption, monitoring and help with audits. CloudBank can also help awardees write a compliance plan.
