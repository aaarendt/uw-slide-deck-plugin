RULE-OF-THUMB CLOUD COSTS. These are round numbers to help you estimate, not quotes; the cost calculators give exact figures. Compute: a virtual machine with about the power of a laptop (2 virtual CPUs, 8 GB of memory, for example AWS m7i.large) costs about 10 cents an hour. A powerful workstation (16 virtual CPUs, 64 GB, for example m7i.4xlarge) is about $1 an hour. A machine with one NVIDIA A10G GPU (for example g5.xlarge) is also about $1 an hour on demand; spot instances, which the cloud can reclaim at short notice, are often about half that. New accounts usually have a GPU quota of zero, so request a quota increase before you need the GPU. Storage: object storage you read often costs about 2.3 cents per GB per month, so a terabyte is about $23 a month; cold archive storage is about 0.1 cents per GB per month, about $1 a terabyte, but takes hours to retrieve and charges for retrieval. Data: downloading data out of the cloud to the internet (egress) costs about 9 cents per GB after the first 100 GB each month, so moving a terabyte out is about $90. Uploading is free. The biggest surprise bills come from things left running: a $1/hour machine left on for a month is about $730.
